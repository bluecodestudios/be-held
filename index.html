01 — The question

How many of us ever get to be held?

You were held once, before you had a name for it. Held by a body, inside water, with nothing asked of you.

Then you were born, and the work of your life began: becoming someone in this world while staying in relationship to the people in it. Nobody tells you that the holding stops. It just does — quietly, somewhere in childhood — and most of us spend the rest of our lives arranging to be useful instead.

Be Held is three hours of the opposite.

02 — Origin

Where this came from

For twelve years a meditation kept returning to me, saying the same thing: this work needs to happen in groups, in water.

It began as floating sound sessions — ten people at a time, floating on a device I built, with Tibetan bowls sounded under the surface. Not crystal bowls. Tibetan ones, because their resonance travels into the body and does something a crystal bowl cannot.

But something kept happening before the water ever came into it. People arrived nervous. Not all of them — some slid into floating like they had been waiting for it — but many needed more attention, more preparation, more reassurance than I had planned for.

And what they needed was not information. It was to be held a little more.
That is when Be Held became its own thing.

03 — The circle

Why this happens in a group

We come from communities that healed together. Circles, rivers, fires, hands. The individualist world taught us to heal alone, in private, on a schedule, and then return to our lives as though nothing happened.

When people arrive together, vulnerable, supported, and in trust, something opens that does not open alone. We become mirrors for one another.

And in the water, something more happens. Water held close to the warmth of our own bodies asks nothing of us. Gravity loosens its grip. The nervous system softens. The guards we would keep up in a circle on land begin to dissolve on their own, and we arrive more open and more grounded than we could ever will ourselves to be.

You are held by me in the water. And you are held by everyone standing where the waves come to rest, who came for the same reason you did and who will take their turn after you.

Here is the part people do not expect: the ones waiting are being held too. A tear, a cry, a smile, laughter, bliss. What surfaces in one body ripples outward until it reaches them, and opens something of their own. No one in this circle is only a spectator.

You stop carrying it alone.
That is the whole medicine.

04 — The session

The three hours

01 / 05 Drag or scroll →

I i — on land

We begin on land

Before anyone touches water, the circle sits together. Intention is spoken — why you came, what you are carrying, what you would like the water to meet. You hear the others speak theirs. By the time anyone gets wet, you are not among strangers anymore.

II ii — entering

We enter together

One by one into warm water, and once the whole circle is in, I guide a meditation to let the group settle — into the water, and into each other.

III iii — in the water

Your turn

I work with each person individually: held, supported, slowly moved. No conversation. Nothing to do. The rest of the circle holds the field while you receive.

IV iv — closing

We close in the water

When the circle is complete, we close together, still in the water.

V v — after

Then we sit

We change, and we sit with tea and fruit. This is not an afterthought. It is where you land, and where the circle puts words to what moved.

05 — If you are nervous

If the idea of floating makes you nervous

Good. Say so when you arrive.

Almost every circle has someone who is uneasy about the water, and in fifteen years I have never met a fear that the first half hour could not work with. You will not be pushed. You will not be surprised. You will not be left to figure anything out on your own.

06 — Who comes

Who comes to Be Held

  1. 01

    The one who has been holding everyone else

    Parents, caregivers, founders, the person everyone leans on. You are very good at holding. Can you remember when was the last time you were held?

  2. 02

    The one carrying something alone

    A grief, a diagnosis, a decision — something you have gotten good at not mentioning.

  3. 03

    The ones who want to do this together

    Friends, families, couples, teams. Be Held works as a private circle for a group who already knows each other.

  4. 04

    The one who is curious and a little scared

    Also welcome. Usually the one who cries.
